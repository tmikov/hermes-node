CLAUDE.md's "Known flaky tests" section describes `test-repl-history.js` as
failing intermittently under the suite's 16-way parallel load and passing in
isolation, and gives a 6-run isolation check as the way to confirm it.

Measured during the VM options work, on this machine, an ASAN build:

- **6/6 passing** in isolation, repeatedly, matching the documented signature.
- **4/8 failing** under 8-way parallel load -- i.e. it reproduces at *half*
  the documented concurrency, and at a rate closer to a coin flip than
  "intermittent" suggests.

Nothing on that branch touched the REPL or anything it depends on, and the
isolation runs rule out a genuine race of the kind `test-fs-async-verify.js`
turned out to have (CLAUDE.md records that one: a flake failing in isolation
is a race worth chasing, and it was).

So this is not a regression. It is sharper data about a known flake, and it
argues the CLAUDE.md note understates it: someone running the suite on a
smaller machine, or with a narrower `-j`, will see this more often than the
current wording leads them to expect.

Two things worth doing, neither urgent:

1. Update the CLAUDE.md note with the 8-way figure, so the next person's
   expectation matches what they will see.
2. The honest state recorded there is still true -- the flake is not
   understood, not fixed, and not quarantined. The 8-way reproduction rate
   makes it cheaper to investigate than it was: 4 failures in 8 runs is a
   usable signal, where a 16-way-only intermittent one is not.

`test-inspect.js`, the other documented flake, did not fire in any run during
this work; its rate was not measured.
