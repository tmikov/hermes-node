`max_wasm_bytes` does not bound what a reader would expect it to bound.
Two separate gaps, both now documented in CLAUDE.md but neither fixed.

**Per-generation, not per-root.** `compileCacheEvictWasm(generationDir_,
...)` walks only the current generation. `kCompileCacheGenerationsKept` is
3, so three older generations survive alongside it, each able to hold its
own budget's worth of Wasm entries. Real on-disk usage can therefore reach
four times `max_wasm_bytes` -- about 1 GB at the 256 MB default. Someone
setting a budget to bound disk use will read the number as the cache's
bound.

**Enforced only on a store.** The sweep runs once per process, on the first
Wasm save. A cache already over budget whose subsequent runs are all hits is
never trimmed. Measured: populate a cache, lower `max_wasm_bytes` to 1000,
run three more times -- all hits, and the 2 MB entry plus its 3.9 MB of
company are still there. So lowering the budget to reclaim disk appears to
do nothing until the next time a new module happens to be compiled.

Neither is a correctness bug and the one-shot-per-process sweep is a
deliberate design choice (it keeps a directory walk off the hot path). But
the combination means the configured number and the observed disk usage can
differ by more than an order of magnitude, which is the kind of gap that
gets reported as a bug.

Options, none evaluated: sweep every generation rather than the current one
(the older ones are pruned by count, so their Wasm bytes are already
untracked); sweep once on cache open as well as on first store, at the cost
of a directory walk per process; or leave both and rely on the
documentation, which is the current state.

Found by the final whole-branch review of the Wasm compile cache
(2026-09-07).
